Stratified Software Development

A Refinement-DAG Calculus for Agent-Authored Systems

Autonomous coding agents fail in three recurring ways: they leap from a requirement to code and invent the missing design; they lose the rationale for load-bearing choices and later weaken tests to make builds pass; and they leave humans with no trustworthy intermediate view of what the system does. This report presents a refinement-DAG calculus that structures software development as an acyclic graph of verified behavior inclusions. One relation — behavior inclusion under a typed edge abstraction map — serves for intent satisfaction, child contract refinement, connector decomposition, and code realization. Stratification is the partial-order acyclicity of the Refinement DAG from root specifications to executable leaves: it turns support evaluation into an acyclic topological evaluation, sibling verification into a substitution lemma, inspection into elementary closure and cut subgraph extraction, and version evolution into proof reuse at unchanged interfaces.
